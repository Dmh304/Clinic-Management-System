II. Software Requirement Specification
1. Overall Requirements
In the context of the digital age, most of the major ophthalmology medical facilities we surveyed have undergone digital transformation, but the old systems are gradually revealing limitations in service and user experience. After surveying the websites of several hospitals and clinics such as Thien Thanh Eye Clinic, Thu Ha Eye Clinic, Tue Anh Eyecare, etc., we can see some limitations as follows:
Overly simple and passive interface: The current system mainly supports registration through static forms, limiting patient interaction.
Slow and passive consultation response: Currently, most clinics still provide consultations through separate platforms such as Zalo, Facebook, or Messenger. Patients have to wait for staff to manually respond to messages, leading to information congestion during peak hours and reducing the user experience. Slow connectivity and synchronization: Results from measuring devices (OCT, refractometer) often require synchronization or manual entry into patient records, easily leading to errors.
The Patient Portal feature is rudimentary: Most systems only offer appointment scheduling and do not provide sufficient medical history information or specialized images for patients to monitor remotely.
Lack of in-depth management reporting: Older systems only provide static data, making it difficult for Clinic Managers to make business decisions based on forecasts.
Inflexible security: Access control is often too loose or too rigid, hindering coordination between specialized ophthalmology departments.
1.1. Context Diagram
The ECMS (Eyes Clinic Management System) is a full-stack web application built for the Anh Sao High-Tech Eye Clinic (85 Ba Trieu St., Hai Ba Trung Dist., Hanoi). The system digitalizes the entire patient lifecycle from appointment scheduling, reception, medical examination, laboratory testing, prescription, medicine dispensing, billing, to reporting.
The system interacts with 7 user actors (Patient, Receptionist, Doctor, Lab Technician, Pharmacist, Clinic Manager, Admin) and 1 system actor (cron jobs, email service). Architecture: ReactJS (Frontend) <-> Spring Boot REST API (Backend) <-> MySQL Database.
1.2. Main Business Processes
1.2.1. Online Appointment Booking & Patient Check-in:
Actors: Patient, Receptionist, System
Objective: Allow patients to register a visit (either online in advance or walk-in at the counter) and complete the check-in procedure that officially places them in the doctor's queue.
Process Summary: 
The process begins with two alternative entry points depending on how the patient initiates their visit.
Online booking path: The patient logs into the Patient Portal, browses available doctors and time slots, and submits a booking request. The system creates a Pending appointment and sends a confirmation email. The receptionist reviews the request: if approved, the appointment is confirmed and the patient is notified; if rejected, the appointment is cancelled with a stated reason and the process ends for that request. On the day of the visit, the patient arrives at the clinic and provides their name or phone number at the counter.
Walk-in path: The patient arrives directly at the clinic and requests an appointment at the reception counter. The receptionist first checks whether the patient already has an account in the system. If an account exists, the receptionist retrieves the existing patient profile. If no account exists, the receptionist registers a new account on the patient's behalf — the system creates the account and sends login credentials to the patient's email. After the account check, the receptionist reviews doctor availability, selects an appropriate time slot, and the system immediately creates a Confirmed appointment with a doctor assigned — bypassing the Pending/approval step entirely.
Both paths converge at the check-in step. The receptionist verifies the patient's identity and performs check-in. The system updates the appointment status to In Progress and pushes the patient into the assigned doctor's queue dashboard.
End State: A formally registered visit exists in the system; the patient appears in the doctor's queue. Hands off to BP-2.
1.2.2. Clinical Examination & EMR Creation:
Actors: Doctor, Lab Technician, System, Patient
Objective: Enable the doctor to conduct a full clinical examination, order and receive lab/imaging results if needed, record a treatment conclusion and medication plan, finalize the Electronic Medical Record (EMR) for the visit, and collect patient feedback.
Process Summary:
The doctor opens their dashboard, selects the next patient from the queue, and reviews the patient's past EMR history before beginning the examination. After conducting the clinical assessment, the doctor enters preliminary symptoms and diagnosis into the EMR form.
If lab tests or imaging are required (e.g., refraction measurement, OCT, ocular ultrasound), the doctor creates a Lab Order. The lab technician receives the order on their dashboard, performs the tests, and submits results along with any attached images. The system links the results to the EMR and pushes them to the doctor in real time. The doctor reviews the results and updates the diagnosis accordingly.
If no lab work is needed, the preliminary diagnosis serves as the official diagnosis and the process continues directly.
At the convergence point of both paths, the doctor writes the treatment conclusion (including the final diagnosis, ICD classification, and treatment direction) and drafts the medication and prescription plan within the EMR. This plan is informational at this stage; the formal Prescription record is created in BP-3.
The doctor then finalizes and submits the EMR. The system saves it as Finalized, locks free editing (audit-protected), unlocks the Prescription feature for this visit, and logs the event to the Audit Log. The system automatically sends a feedback request to the patient via the Portal and email. The patient may optionally rate the service quality and leave a comment; if submitted, the feedback is saved and the Clinic Manager is notified.
End State: The EMR is permanently finalized. Hands off to BP-3 if medication is needed, or ends the clinical flow otherwise.
1.2.3. Prescription & Medicine Dispensing:
Actors: Doctor, Pharmacist, Patient, System
Objective: Transfer the doctor's prescription to the pharmacy, give the patient the choice to purchase medications, dispense the correct drugs if the patient opts in, and trigger invoice generation for the relevant charges.
Process Summary:
After the EMR is finalized, the doctor opens the prescription form. Two tasks may run in parallel: writing the medicine prescription (drug name, dosage, frequency, duration) and, if applicable, entering optical parameters for an eyeglass prescription (SPH, CYL, AXIS, ADD, PD per eye). The system saves the eyeglass prescription and generates a shareable PDF for the patient.
Once both tasks are complete, the doctor reviews the full prescription and submits it. The system saves it and notifies the patient to decide on purchase.
The patient then decides whether to purchase the prescribed medications. If they choose to buy, the pharmacist receives the order on the Pharmacy Dashboard, reviews the prescription lines, prepares each item, and confirms dispensing. The system updates the prescription status to Dispensed, generates a PharmacyInvoice, and notifies the receptionist to include pharmacy charges in the final bill. The patient collects the medications at the pharmacy counter, and the Patient Portal is updated accordingly.
If the patient chooses not to purchase medications, the system marks the prescription as Skipped and notifies the receptionist that only examination fees apply for billing.
End State: The prescription is either Dispensed or Skipped; the appropriate invoice components are ready. Hands off to BP-4.
1.2.4. Payment Processing & Invoice Generation:
Actors: Receptionist, Patient, System
Objective: Consolidate all charges from the visit into a single invoice, process payment through the patient's chosen method, issue an electronic receipt, and officially close the visit.
Process Summary:
After the patient has completed examination and received medications (if applicable), the receptionist opens the invoice screen for the visit. The system automatically aggregates all applicable charges: service/examination fee, lab fees (if any), and pharmacy fees (if medications were dispensed), then displays an itemized breakdown. The receptionist reviews the breakdown, applies any discounts or insurance adjustments, and confirms invoice creation. The system creates an Unpaid invoice and displays the total.
The receptionist informs the patient of the total amount and asks for the preferred payment method. For cash payment, the patient pays at the counter, the receptionist records it, and the system marks the invoice as Paid. For online payment (VietQR / bank QR transfer), the patient completes the transaction and the system waits for a callback from the payment gateway: on success, the invoice is marked Paid and the patient is notified via the Portal; on failure or timeout, the status is set to Payment Failed and the patient is prompted to retry.
Once payment is confirmed, two actions run in parallel: the system generates and sends an e-invoice PDF to the patient's email and Portal while also recording the revenue data for reporting; and the receptionist prints a paper invoice if the patient requests one. Finally, the system updates the appointment status to Completed and writes the full event chain to the Audit Log.
End State: Invoice issued, payment recorded, visit closed with status Completed.
1.2.5. System Administration & RBAC Configuration:
Actors: Admin, New Staff, System
Objective: Allow the system administrator to create staff accounts, assign roles, configure access permissions via Role-Based Access Control (RBAC), and ensure new staff can log in securely and land on the correct dashboard for their role.
Process Summary:
The admin creates a new staff account by entering the employee's name, email, role, and department. The system validates that the email is not already registered, creates the account in an Inactive state, and generates a temporary password.
The admin then opens the Roles & Permissions screen to review the default permission matrix for the assigned role. If the default configuration is sufficient, the admin proceeds directly to activation. If custom permissions are needed  (for example, granting or restricting access to specific modules beyond the role's defaults) the admin adjusts the permission matrix. The system saves the custom RBAC configuration and writes the changes (including admin ID, timestamp, and specific permissions modified) to the Audit Log.
The admin activates the account. The system sets the account to Active and sends a welcome email to the new staff member containing the temporary password and login instructions.
The new staff member logs in with the temporary password. The system authenticates the credentials, detects that this is a first login, and forces a password change. The staff member sets a new password compliant with the security policy. The system invalidates the temporary password, issues a JWT and refresh token, redirects the user to their role-specific dashboard, and records both the first login and the password change event to the Audit Log.
End State: The staff account is fully active with correct RBAC configuration; the staff member is authenticated and working within their designated dashboard.

1.3.3. Use Case Diagrams
1.3.3.1. UCs for Guest
In the ECMS system, the Guest is an actor representing unauthenticated users—meaning any individual who accesses the system without an account or before logging in. This use case diagram defines the scope of public interactions available to Guests, which includes viewing informational content on the system and registering for an account to become an authenticated user. Notably, the two «extend» relationships in the diagram reflect the optional behaviors of the Guest: viewing the details of a blog post or looking up a specific doctor's information is only triggered when the Guest actively selects that option, rather than being a mandatory action in every interaction session.
1.3.3.2. UCs for User
In the ECMS system, the User is an actor representing authenticated users—meaning individuals who have successfully registered and logged into their accounts. This use case diagram defines the scope of core interactions available to the User, which includes performing Login/Logout, Register, Change password, Manage personal profile, and Receive system notifications.
The generalization relationship from User to Guest indicates that the User inherently inherits and can perform all public capabilities available to a Guest. Notably, the «extend» relationships in the diagram reflect conditional or optional behaviors within a session: resetting a forgotten password is only triggered from the login process, whereas actions like changing the password or updating specific details (Update personal profile) serve as extended behavioral branches tied to the personal profile management process whenever the User requires them.
1.3.3.3. UCs for Patient
In the ECMS system, the Patient is a specialized actor representing customers who utilize the medical and healthcare services of the system. The generalization relationship from Patient to User indicates that the Patient inherently inherits and is capable of performing all core functionalities available to a standard User. This use case diagram defines the scope of advanced interactions dedicated to the Patient, which includes booking appointments online (Book appointment online), viewing medical history (View medical history), and interacting with the support chatbot (Interact with chatbot).
The system provides conditional and optional behavioral flows through «extend» relationships to optimize the patient experience. Specifically, from the appointment management process (Manage patient's appointment), the Patient can optionally view appointment details (View appointment detail) or reschedule/cancel the appointment (Rebook/cancel appointment). Regarding result lookups, viewing laboratory results (View Lab result) can extend to examining specific eye measurements. Notably, viewing overall examination results (View examination results) serves as a foundational use case that flexibly extends to viewing laboratory results, viewing electronic prescriptions and eyeglasses (View Prescriptions), or viewing diagnosis results (View diagnosis result) depending on the actual context of the medical session. Finally, within the financial workflow, while viewing the invoice list (View Invoice list), the Patient can choose to view specific invoice details (View Invoice detail), which subsequently triggers the online payment action (Pay invoice) whenever they need to settle the expenses.
1.3.3.3. UCs for Doctor
In the ECMS system, the Doctor is a specialized actor representing medical professionals responsible for performing clinical and specialized healthcare operations. The generalization relationship from Doctor to User indicates that the Doctor inherently inherits and is capable of performing all core functionalities available to a standard User. This use case diagram defines the scope of advanced privileges and interactions dedicated to the Doctor, which includes managing Electronic Medical Records (Manage Electronic Medical Record - EMR), issuing drug or eyeglass prescriptions (Issue Drug/Glass Prescription), viewing laboratory results (View lab result), viewing patient medical information (View Patient Medical Information), and viewing their personal schedule (View personal schedule).
To ensure that medical workflows are both rigorous and flexible, the system establishes supplementary relationships between use cases. Specifically, from the EMR management process, the Doctor can optionally choose to create a new record (Create new EMR) or update an existing one (Update existing EMR) via «extend» relationships. Within the examination and prescription workflow, issuing a drug/glass prescription (Issue Drug/Glass Prescription) mandatorily encompasses recording clinical diagnoses and notes (Record Clinical Diagnosis and Notes) through an «include» relationship. Concurrently, this prescription process can be extended by recording and submitting visual acuity test results (Record and Submit Visual Acuity Test Results) or by viewing laboratory results (View lab result) based on clinical needs. The view lab result use case itself inherently incorporates ordering a laboratory test (Order Lab Test) via an «include» relationship. Furthermore, when accessing patient medical information (View Patient Medical Information), the Doctor can optionally update medical details (Update patient medical information) or review the patient's comprehensive medical history (View Patient Medical History). Finally, from the personal schedule interface, the Doctor can actively choose to reschedule or cancel appointments (Reschedule/cancel schedule) depending on their actual availability.
1.3.3.4. UCs for Receptionist 
In the ECMS system, the Receptionist is a specialized actor representing front-desk staff responsible for coordinating appointments, welcoming patients, and handling initial financial transactions at the clinic. The generalization relationship from Receptionist to User indicates that the Receptionist inherently inherits and is capable of performing all core functionalities available to a standard User. This use case diagram defines the operational scope dedicated to the Receptionist, which includes managing patient appointments (Manage patient's appointment), registering walk-in patients (Register walk-in patient), viewing the overall appointment schedule (View appointment schedule), collecting examination fees and issuing invoices (Process Payment), and sending appointment reminder notifications (Send Appointment Reminder Notification).
The coordination and operational workflows are optimized through supplementary relationships between use cases. Specifically, from the patient appointment management process, the Receptionist can optionally choose to view appointment details (View appointment detail), reschedule or cancel an appointment (Rebook/cancel appointment), or confirm the appointment and assign it to a doctor (Confirm appointment) via «extend» relationships. Regarding schedule monitoring, from the view appointment schedule interface, the staff can optionally choose to reschedule or cancel a specific schedule (Reschedule/cancel schedule). Within the financial workflow, once the examination fee collection and invoice generation (process payment) are completed, the system allows for an extended action to print or send an electronic invoice to the patient (Deliver Invoice). Finally, to support attendance management, the action of sending appointment reminder notifications (Send Appointment Reminder Notification) mandatorily encompasses and incorporates data from the prior booking process (Book appointment) through an «include» relationship.
1.3.3.5. UCs for Lab technician
In the ECMS system, the Lab technician is a specialized actor representing laboratory personnel responsible for receiving orders, performing specialized measurements, and updating test results in the system. The generalization relationship from Lab technician to User indicates that the Lab technician inherently inherits and is capable of performing all core functionalities available to a standard User. This use case diagram defines the specialized operational scope dedicated to the Lab technician, which comprises two primary interactions: submitting laboratory results (Submit lab result) and viewing the laboratory queue (View lab queue).
The system establishes mandatory integrated workflows and extended behavioral branches to optimize accuracy within the laboratory environment. Specifically, regarding the result handling workflow, the action of submitting laboratory results (Submit lab result) mandatorily encompasses viewing the doctor's order (View doctor's order) for verification and inherently incorporates the process of recording the laboratory results (Record lab result) before final submission through «include» relationships. Furthermore, from this submission process, the technician can optionally choose to modify data via the update laboratory result use case (Update lab result) through an «extend» relationship when corrections are needed. Within the operational workflow, from the view laboratory queue interface (View lab queue) used to monitor waiting patients, the system provides an extended capability to filter or search for specific patients (Filter/search patient) to expedite data lookup and coordination.
1.3.3.6. UCs for Pharmacist
In the ECMS system, the Pharmacist is a specialized actor representing pharmacy personnel responsible for managing, dispensing medications, and handling pharmaceutical-related invoices at the clinic's pharmacy. The generalization relationship from Pharmacist to User indicates that the Pharmacist inherently inherits and is capable of performing all core functionalities available to a standard User. This use case diagram defines the specialized operational scope dedicated to the Pharmacist, which comprises two primary interactions: dispensing medication (Dispense Drug) and issuing electronic invoices (Issue Electronic Invoice).
The system establishes mandatory verification workflows and flexible extended paths to ensure accuracy during the pharmaceutical dispensing process. Specifically, within the dispensing workflow, the action of dispensing medication (Dispense Drug) mandatorily encompasses viewing the drug prescription (View drug prescription) via an «include» relationship to cross-check clinical orders. From this dispensing process, the system allows for an extended capability to view patient requirements (View patient requirement) through an «extend» relationship. While accessing the patient requirement interface, the pharmacist can optionally approve or decline the drug prescription (Approve/Decline drug prescription) based on stock availability or prescription validity. Within the financial workflow, once the electronic invoice generation (Issue Electronic Invoice) is completed, the process can be extended to the invoice payment action (Pay invoice) when the pharmacist assists the patient in settling the transaction at the counter.
1.3.3.7. UCs for Clinic Manager
In the ECMS system, the Clinic manager is a high-level actor representing the clinic's management board, responsible for overseeing global operational activities, evaluating staff performance, and analyzing comprehensive financial metrics. The generalization relationship from Clinic manager to User indicates that the Clinic manager inherently inherits and is capable of performing all core functionalities available to a standard User. This use case diagram defines the administrative scope dedicated to the Clinic manager, which comprises generating revenue reports (Generate Revenue Report), monitoring the staff performance dashboard (Monitor Staff Performance Dashboard), viewing the real-time operational analytics dashboard (View Real-time Operational Analytics Dashboard), viewing the daily appointment schedule (View Daily Appointment Schedule), and generating feedback reports (Generate feedback report).
The system establishes rigorous data aggregation workflows to support strategic decision-making. Specifically, within the financial and operational reporting workflow, the action of generating a revenue report (Generate Revenue Report) mandatorily encompasses three integral subprocesses: viewing the number of service package subscriptions (View the number of service package subscriptions), viewing patient volume statistics and trends (View Patient Volume Statistics and Trends), and viewing inventory and pharmacy consumption reports (View Inventory and Pharmacy Consumption Report) via «include» relationships. Within the schedule management workflow, from the view daily appointment schedule interface, the manager can optionally choose to reschedule or cancel a schedule (Reschedule/Cancelled schedule) through an «extend» relationship when emergency reallocations are required. Finally, regarding service quality administration, the process of generating a feedback report (Generate feedback report) mandatorily incorporates inspecting specific patient reviews (View patient's feedback) through an «include» relationship.
1.3.3.8. UCs for Admin
In the ECMS system, the Admin is the highest-level actor representing the system administrator, responsible for managing the technical infrastructure, ensuring data security, and maintaining the overall stability of the platform. The generalization relationship from Admin to User indicates that the Admin inherently inherits and is capable of performing all core functionalities available to a standard User. This use case diagram defines the core administrative scope dedicated to the Admin, which comprises three primary platform interactions: managing user accounts (Manage User Account), configuring system and data (Configure System and Data), and managing system audit logs (Manage System Audit Log).
The system establishes strict security control workflows combined with flexible redundancy mechanisms through supplementary relationships between use cases. Specifically, within the user administration workflow, the action of managing user accounts (Manage User Account) mandatorily encompasses modifying role-based permissions (Modify Role-Based Permissions - RBAC) via an «include» relationship to guarantee system security. Concurrently, from this account management process, the Admin can optionally choose to deactivate accounts (Deactive account) through an «extend» relationship upon detecting violations. Regarding infrastructure administration, from the configure system and data interface, the workflow can be extended to the action of restoring system data from backup (Restore System Data from Backup) in the event of system failures. Finally, within the audit workflow, the feature of managing system audit logs (Manage System Audit Log) provides an extended capability to configure automated backup schedules (Configure Automated Backup Schedule) via an «extend» relationship to optimize routine data safety.

UC ID & Name
UC-01: Register User Account
Primary Actor
Guest
Secondary Actors
System (email verification service)
Trigger
Guest clicks the 'Register' button on the Login/Register screen.
Description
A Guest fills in the registration form with personal details (full name, email, password, phone number, date of birth). The system creates a new Patient account with status = PENDING_VERIFICATION and sends an email verification link to activate the account.
Preconditions
PRE-1: The guest accesses the public Login/Register screen.
PRE-2: The email address provided is not already registered in the system.
Postconditions
POST-1: A new record is created in the users table with role = PATIENT and status = PENDING_VERIFICATION.
POST-2: The patient receives an email with a time-limited activation link.
POST-3: After clicking the link, account status transitions to ACTIVE.
Normal Flow
1. Guest navigates to the Login/Register screen and selects 'Register'.
2. Guest fills in: full name, email, password, confirm password, phone number, date of birth.
3. System validates all required fields and format rules (E1, E2).
4. System hashes the password using bcrypt (cost ≥ 12) and creates the account record.
5. System sends a verification email containing a one-time activation link (valid 24 hours).
6. Guest opens the email and clicks the activation link.
7. System verifies the token, sets account status = ACTIVE.
8. System redirects the guest to the Login screen with a success message.
Alternative Flows
None
Exceptions
E1 — Email already registered:
System displays 'This email is already registered. Please log in.' Guest is redirected to the Login screen.
 
E2 — Password does not meet policy (BR-01):
System highlights the password field and displays: 'Password must be at least 8 characters, including uppercase, lowercase, and a digit.' Guest corrects and resubmits.
 
E3 — Activation link expired:
System displays: 'This activation link has expired. Request a new one?' Guest may request a fresh link.
Priority
Must Have
Frequency of Use
Low; primarily during initial patient onboarding
Business Rules
BR-01 (Password Policy)
Assumptions
Guest has a valid email address. SMTP service is available.

   - Doctor → EMR Dashboard
   - Receptionist → Appointment Management
   - Lab Technician → Lab Queue
   - Pharmacist → Dispensing Dashboard
   - Clinic Manager → Reports Dashboard
   - Admin → Admin Panel
Alternative Flows

2. System redirects to Google Identity Platform.

5. If matched, proceed from normal flow step 3.
6. If no match, system pre-fills registration form and prompts account creation.
Exceptions
E1 — Incorrect password:
System increments login_fail_count. If count = 5 within 30 minutes, account is locked for 30 minutes (BR-02). System displays remaining attempts.
 
E2 — Account locked (BR-02):
System displays: 'Account locked. Try again in X minutes, or contact support.'
 
E3 — Email not verified:
System displays: 'Please verify your email first.' with option to resend the verification link.
Priority
Must Have
Frequency of Use
Very high; every user session
Business Rules
BR-01 (Password Policy), BR-02 (Account Locking)
Assumptions
SMTP available for re-send verification emails. Google OAuth credentials configured for Should Have scope.

UC-18: Reassign Appointment
UC ID & Name
UC-18: Reassign Appointment
Primary Actor

Secondary Actors

Trigger
Clinic Manager (or a Receptionist escalating to the Manager) needs to move an appointment to a different doctor and/or time slot — e.g., doctor sick leave, schedule conflict, or daily quota overflow (BR-03).
Description
The Clinic Manager reassigns one or more appointments from their originally booked doctor/slot to a different doctor and/or time slot, preserving the patient's booking history and notifying all affected parties.
Preconditions
PRE-01: The Clinic Manager is authenticated.
PRE-02: An appointment with status PENDING, CONFIRMED, WAITING, or IN_PROGRESS exists on the selected date.
PRE-03: If a new doctor is supplied, that doctor must exist and have remaining capacity on the resulting date (BR-03). 
Postconditions
POST-01: The appointment is updated with the new doctor and/or new date/time, according to which ever fields were changed.
POST-02: The reassignment reason is appended to the appointment's history notes; existing notes are preserved.
POST-03: Notifications are sent to the patient, the previously assigned doctor (if the doctor changed), and the newly assigned doctor (if a doctor is involved). 
Normal Flow
1. The Clinic Manager selects a date and reviews the list of appointments scheduled for that day.
2. The Clinic Manager selects an appointment to reassign and provides at least one of the following: a new doctor, a new date/time, or a reassignment reason.
3. The system validates that the appointment is not in a final state (BR-22).
4. If a new doctor is provided, the system verifies that the doctor exists and has not reached the daily appointment limit (BR-03).
5. The system applies the changes and appends the reassignment reason to the appointment's history notes without overwriting existing content (BR-09).
6. The system notifies all affected parties — the patient, the previously assigned doctor, and the newly assigned doctor — as applicable (BR-06).
7. The appointment list is refreshed to reflect the updated schedule.

Time-only change. 
A1. The Clinic Manager provides only a new date/time without changing the doctor.
A2. The existing doctor is notified of the updated schedule for their patient. 
Alternative Flows B
Doctor-only change. 
B1. The Clinic Manager provides only a new doctor without changing the date/time.
B2. Both the previously assigned doctor and the newly assigned doctor are notified. The patient is informed of the new doctor. 
Exceptions
EX-01 (Non-reassignable state): The system prevents reassignment of appointments already marked as COMPLETED or CANCELLED and displays an error message.
EX-02 (Doctor not found): The system alerts the Clinic Manager if the specified doctor does not exist or is no longer active in the system.
EX-03 (Doctor over capacity): The system blocks the reassignment if the target doctor has reached the daily appointment limit and prompts the Clinic Manager to select an alternative.
EX-04 (Notification failure): The system continues with the reassignment even if automated notification dispatch fails, ensuring the clinical schedule is updated. 
Priority
Should Have
Frequency of Use
Occasional; spikes during doctor absences or schedule conflicts.
Business Rules
BR-03: Doctor appointment daily capacity is enforced on reassignment exactly as it is on patient-initiated reschedule.
BR-06: Both the previously assigned doctor and the newly assigned doctor must be notified whenever the assigned doctor changes.
BR-09: The reassignment reason is appended to existing appointment notes, preserving the full audit trail.
BR-22: Only appointments in a non-final state (PENDING, CONFIRMED, WAITING, IN_PROGRESS) may be reassigned. 
Assumptions
Doctor schedule capacity is maintained and updated in real time by the system.The system clock is synchronized to the clinic's local time zone (UTC+7). 
Relations to other UCs

UC-39: Purchase Service Package
Primary Actor

Trigger
User navigates to the 'Service Packages' section on the Home Page or Patient Portal.

Any visitor (Guest) can view the clinic's published wellness/service package catalogue (e.g., eye massage, eye meditation, care bundles) with names, descriptions, and general benefit overviews — without login. An authenticated Patient can select and purchase a package online via VietQR/bank transfer; upon successful payment the package is activated and linked to their account as a ServiceSubscription. Patients may then book individual sessions from the package via UC-11.
Preconditions
PRE-01: At least one active service package exists in the catalog, each categorized as either a clinical or a care service.
PRE-02: The catalog page is publicly accessible without authentication. 
Postconditions
POST-01 (CARE service, Patient): A service registration is created with status PENDING, linking the package to the patient's account and making it visible under the patient's registered services.
POST-02 (CARE service, Receptionist on behalf): Same as POST-01, with the patient identity and optional notes supplied by the Receptionist.
POST-03 (CLINICAL service, Patient): No registration is created; the Patient is redirected into the appointment booking flow with the service pre-selected. 
Normal Flow
1. The actor opens the service catalog page.
2. The system displays all active service packages as cards showing name, category, description, duration, number of sessions, price, and thumbnail.
3. The actor can search by keyword or category, or switch between grid and list views.
4. The actor selects a package card to view its full details.
5. If the actor is unauthenticated, the system prompts them to log in before proceeding.
6. If the actor is an authenticated Patient and the package is a CARE service, the system requests confirmation to register; upon confirmation a registration record is created and a success message is shown.
7. If the actor is an authenticated Patient and the package is a CLINICAL service, the system redirects to the appointment booking flow with the service pre-selected (see UC-11).
8. If the actor is a Receptionist and the package is a CARE service, the system prompts the Receptionist to search for and identify the target patient and optionally add notes, then confirms registration on the patient's behalf. 
Alternative Flows
Already-pending registration. 
A1. The Patient already has a pending registration for the same service.
A2. The registration action is disabled and the system informs the Patient that a pending registration already exists, preventing a duplicate submission. 
Exceptions
EX-01 (Registration fails): The system displays an error message and keeps the registration form open for retry.
EX-02 (No patient selected, Receptionist flow): The system prevents the Receptionist from confirming registration until a patient has been identified via search.
EX-03 (Catalogue empty or no search matches): The system displays an appropriate empty-state message indicating no services are available or no results were found. 
Priority
Should Have
Frequency of Use
Occasional; expected spike during promotional campaigns.
Business Rules
BR-01: Browsing the catalog requires no authentication; registering for a CARE package or booking a CLINICAL service requires the Patient role, or a Receptionist acting on a patient's behalf.
BR-02: The service category determines the action — CLINICAL services always route into the appointment booking flow; CARE services go through the registration funnel.
BR-03: A Receptionist registering on behalf of a patient must first locate that patient via search; no new patient may be created from this screen. 
Assumptions
The service package catalog is maintained by the Clinic Manager.
Payment configuration is managed separately by the system administrator. 
Non-functional requirements
NFR-01 (Performance): catalogue list must load within < 2 seconds at P95 for a public, unauthenticated visitor.
NFR-02 (Availability): the catalogue is publicly cacheable since it requires no auth, but registration/booking actions enforce role checks server-side regardless of UI button state.

Receptionist (books on behalf of a patient). 
Trigger
Patient selects 'Book a Session' from an active service-package subscription on 'My Packages' (UC-40), or selects a single non-medical care service directly from the catalogue.
Description
A Patient with an active service-package subscription (or purchasing a standalone session) schedules a date/time for a wellness/care session (e.g., eye massage, eye meditation). Unlike a doctor appointment, the session does not require a doctor and is later staffed by a Nurse.
Preconditions
PRE-01: The Patient holds an active service-package subscription with at least one remaining session.
PRE-02: The subscription's expired date, if set, has not passed. 
Postconditions
POST-01: A care session record is created with status BOOKED, assigned the next sequential session number for that subscription, the chosen date/time, and any optional notes.
POST-02: The subscription's used session count is incremented and the remaining session count is decremented immediately at booking time.
POST-03: If the remaining session count reaches zero, the subscription status is automatically updated to DEPLETED.
POST-04: An in-app notification is sent to the patient confirming the booking. 
Normal Flow
1. The Patient opens their care session booking page, optionally pre-filled from a specific subscription.
2. The system lists the Patient's active subscriptions that still have remaining sessions.
3. The Patient selects a subscription, picks a future date and time, and optionally enters notes.
4. The Patient submits the booking request.
5. The system validates that the selected subscription is active, not expired, and has remaining sessions, and that the chosen date and time is in the future.
6. The system creates the care session, deducts one session from the subscription balance, and marks the subscription as DEPLETED if no sessions remain.
7. The system sends a confirmation notification to the patient and confirms the booking. 
Alternative Flows
Receptionist books on behalf of a patient. 
A1. The Receptionist opens the booking form for an identified patient's active subscription.
A2. The same validation and session-deduction rules apply; the resulting session and notification are attributed to the patient. 
Exceptions
EX-01 (Missing subscription): The system prompts the actor to select a valid subscription before proceeding.
EX-02 (Missing date/time): The system prompts the actor to provide a valid date and time.
EX-03 (Past date/time): The system rejects the booking and informs the actor that the scheduled time must be in the future.
EX-04 (Subscription not active): The system informs the actor that the selected subscription is no longer active.
EX-05 (Subscription expired): The system informs the actor that the subscription has passed its expiry date and marks it as EXPIRED.
EX-06 (No remaining sessions): The system informs the actor that all sessions in the subscription have been used.
EX-07 (Ownership mismatch): The system rejects the request if a Patient attempts to book against a subscription that does not belong to them. 
Priority
Should Have
Frequency of Use
Occasional; tied to active subscription volume.
Business Rules
BR-01: Sessions are deducted from the subscription balance at booking time, not at session-delivery time, to prevent overbooking beyond the purchased balance.
BR-02: The session number is assigned sequentially based on the patient's existing booked sessions for that subscription.
BR-03: A subscription automatically transitions from ACTIVE to DEPLETED once all sessions are consumed, and from ACTIVE to EXPIRED the first time a booking is attempted past its expiry date. 
Assumptions
Care-session capacity per time slot is configured by the Clinic Manager; nurse staffing is resolved afterward, not at booking time.
Non-functional requirements
NFR-01 (Performance): subscription validation and CareSession creation must complete within < 2 seconds at P95.
NFR-02 (Concurrency): the session-deduction check and CareSession insert should be treated as one atomic unit to avoid a double-booking race against the same balance.

UC-55: Manage User Account

Description
The Admin creates, views, edits, activates, and deactivates staff accounts (Doctor, Receptionist, Lab Technician, Pharmacist, Manager). For Patient accounts — created via self-registration or walk-in registration — the Admin has read access plus the ability to activate/deactivate (e.g., on patient request, suspected fraud, or duplicate-account cleanup). Admin cannot create a Patient account directly through this use case. Hard deletion is prohibited for all account types (BR-09). 
Preconditions
PRE-1: Admin is authenticated.
Postconditions
POST-1: Account changes persisted.
POST-2: New staff account email sent if a new staff account was created.
POST-3: Event recorded in Audit Log.
POST-4: If a Patient account is deactivated, all active sessions/tokens for that account are invalidated and any pending appointments remain unaffected unless separately cancelled. 
Normal Flow
1. Admin navigates to User Management.
2. Admin selects account type filter: Staff or Patient.
3. (Staff path) Admin clicks 'Add New User', enters full name, email, role, department.
4. System validates the email is not already registered (E1).
5. System creates the account (status = INACTIVE) with a temporary password.
6. Admin reviews the default RBAC permissions for the assigned role.
7. Admin clicks 'Activate Account'.
8. System sets status = ACTIVE and sends a welcome email with temporary credentials.
9. Event is recorded in the Audit Log. 
Alternative Flows
ALT-1 — Deactivate Account (Staff or Patient):
1. Admin selects an active account and clicks 'Deactivate'.
2. System sets status = INACTIVE and invalidates all active tokens.
3. Event logged.
ALT-2 — Edit Account Details (Staff only):
1. Admin selects a staff account and modifies: name, role, department.
2. System saves changes and logs the event.
ALT-3 — Search and View Patient Account:
1. Admin switches filter to "Patient" and searches by name, phone number, or patient code.

1. Admin selects an inactive Patient account and clicks 'Reactivate'.
2. System sets status = ACTIVE.
3. Event logged; optional notification email sent to the patient.

E2 — Attempt to create a Patient account via Admin UI: System displays "Patient accounts must be created via self-registration or walk-in check-in" and blocks the action.

Must Have

5. Appendix
5.1 Assumptions & Dependencies
AS-1: The clinic has a stable internet connection to use the web-based system.
AS-2: Patients have valid email addresses to receive notifications and electronic invoices.
AS-3: Existing patient data will be manually entered or imported from Excel during the deployment phase; there will be no automatic migration in v0.
DE-1: The system relies on the SMTP service (Gmail SMTP / SendGrid) for email sending. If SMTP is unavailable, appointment reminders and invoice sending will be suspended.
DE-2: Google OAuth 2.0 login functionality relies on the Google Identity Platform (Should Have).
DE-3: Cloud deployment (AWS EC2 / Railway) or VPS with HTTPS support.
5.2 Limitations & Exclusions
The following features are outside the scope of version 0:
• Does not integrate with the national health insurance system.
• Does not support telemedicine/remote consultations.
• Does not integrate automatic vision testing devices (electronic refractometers, automatic tonometers).
• Only supports single-clinic; does not yet support multi-branch clinic chains.
• Does not yet support private insurance payments.

Electronic Medical Record
EMR
Electronic Medical Record (EMR) is a digital version of a patient's paper chart that contains their medical history, diagnoses, and treatment plans within a single healthcare practice.

VA
Visual Acuity without correction  (VA) is the natural sharpness of a person's vision measured without the use of any corrective glasses or contact lenses. 
Best Corrected Visual Acuity
BCVA
Best Corrected Visual Acuity (BCVA) is the sharpest possible vision a person can achieve while wearing the optimal prescription glasses or contact lenses. 
Intraocular Pressure
IOP
The pressure inside the eye, known as intraocular pressure (IOP), is measured in millimeters of mercury (mmHg). 
Optical Coherence Tomography
OCT
Optical Coherence Tomography (OCT) is a non-invasive imaging technique that visualizes the eye's microvasculature in 3D without the need for dye injections.
